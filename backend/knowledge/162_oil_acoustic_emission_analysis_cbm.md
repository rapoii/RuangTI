## 1. Introduction to Multi-Modal CBM

While vibration analysis (Module 161) dominates rotating machinery diagnostics, it has blind spots: low-speed equipment, early-stage lubrication failure, and micro-crack initiation often produce negligible vibration energy. **Oil Analysis** and **Acoustic Emission (AE)** fill these gaps by monitoring chemical degradation and high-frequency stress waves respectively. Integrating these modalities creates a robust Condition-Based Maintenance (CBM) strategy that detects faults across the entire P-F curve interval.

---

## 2. Oil Analysis: Tribological Diagnostics

Oil analysis treats the lubricant as a "blood sample" of the machine. It provides three categories of information:

### 2.1 Lubricant Condition Monitoring
Degradation mechanisms include oxidation, nitration, and additive depletion. Key indicators:

-   **Total Acid Number (TAN):** Measures acidic byproducts of oxidation.
    $$
    \text{TAN} = \frac{(A - B) \times N \times 56.1}{W} \quad [\text{mg KOH/g}]
    $$
    Where $A$ = sample titration volume, $B$ = blank titration volume, $N$ = normality of KOH, $W$ = sample weight. Alert limits typically trigger at TAN > 2× baseline.

-   **Viscosity Index (VI):** Quantifies viscosity change with temperature.
    $$
    VI = \frac{L - U}{L - H} \times 100
    $$
    Where $U$ = kinematic viscosity at 40°C, $L$ and $H$ are reference oils with VI=0 and VI=100 respectively. A drop >10% indicates shear thinning or contamination.

### 2.2 Wear Particle Analysis
Ferrography and spectrometry identify wear modes:

| Technique | Size Range | Detectable Elements | Application |
|-----------|------------|---------------------|-------------|
| Rotrode Filter Spectroscopy | <8 µm | Fe, Cu, Pb, Sn, Al | Early wear trending |
| Analytical Ferrography | 1–1000 µm | Morphology-based | Severe sliding, fatigue, corrosion |
| Particle Counting (ISO 4406) | 4–100 µm | Concentration | Hydraulic cleanliness |

**Wear Rate Modeling:** The Archard wear equation relates particle generation rate $\dot{V}$ to operating conditions:
$$
\dot{V} = K \frac{W L}{H}
$$
Where $K$ = wear coefficient, $W$ = load, $L$ = sliding distance, $H$ = hardness. In oil analysis, $\dot{V}$ is estimated via ppm/hr trends corrected for oil make-up volume.

### 2.3 Contamination Detection
-   **Water Activity ($a_w$):** Karl Fischer titration measures dissolved water. Saturation point varies with oil age; $a_w > 0.8$ risks free water formation.
-   **Silicon (Si):** Indicates dirt ingestion. Si/Al ratio distinguishes airborne dust (~5:1) from sealant/gasket material (~1:1).

## 3. Acoustic Emission (AE) Analysis

AE detects transient elastic waves (20 kHz – 1 MHz) generated by rapid energy release events: crack propagation, friction, phase transformation, and cavitation. Unlike vibration (structural response), AE is a *source* phenomenon.

### 3.1 Signal Parameters
AE signals are characterized in time and frequency domains:

-   **Hit Rate:** Counts per second exceeding threshold.
-   **Amplitude:** Peak voltage in dB$_{AE}$ ($0 \text{ dB} = 1 \mu V$ at sensor).
-   **Energy:** Area under rectified signal envelope.
    $$
    E = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} |V(t)|^2 dt
    $$
-   **RMS Voltage:** Continuous indicator for rubbing/friction.
-   **Average Frequency:** $AF = \frac{\text{Counts}}{\text{Duration}}$

### 3.2 Source Location
For linear structures (pipes, rails), source location uses Time-of-Arrival (TOA):
$$
x = \frac{v_g (t_2 - t_1) + d}{2}
$$
Where $v_g$ = group velocity, $t_1, t_2$ = arrival times at sensors separated by distance $d$. For 2D planar location, minimum 3 sensors solve:
$$
(x - x_i)^2 + (y - y_i)^2 = v_g^2 (t_i - t_0)^2 \quad i=1,2,3
$$

### 3.3 Applications in CBM
-   **Rolling Element Bearings:** AE detects surface distress *before* vibration signatures appear. The defect frequency matches BPFO/BPFI but appears in the 100–300 kHz band, avoiding structural noise.
-   **Gear Tooth Cracking:** Burst-type AE events correlate with tooth root crack propagation. Energy per mesh cycle increases monotonically with crack depth.
-   **Cavitation in Pumps:** Broadband RMS elevation precedes head loss. Useful for detecting NPSH margin erosion.
-   **Leak Detection:** Turbulent flow through orifices generates continuous AE. Sensitivity down to 0.01 l/min in pressurized systems.

## 4. Sensor Fusion & Decision Logic

Combining oil, AE, and vibration requires hierarchical decision frameworks:

### 4.1 Dempster-Shafer Evidence Theory
Handles conflicting evidence without requiring prior probabilities. Basic Probability Assignment (BPA) $m(A)$ satisfies:
$$
\sum_{A \subseteq \Theta} m(A) = 1, \quad m(\emptyset) = 0
$$
Combined belief from two independent sources:
$$
m_{12}(C) = \frac{1}{1-K} \sum_{A \cap B = C} m_1(A) m_2(B)
$$
Where conflict $K = \sum_{A \cap B = \emptyset} m_1(A) m_2(B)$. High $K$ triggers manual review rather than automated diagnosis.

## 5. Implementation Standards & Best Practices

-   **Oil Sampling:** ISO 4021 (extraction), ISO 3722 (container cleanliness). Sample from live flow zones, never from sump bottom dead zones.
-   **AE Testing:** ASTM E976 (sensor mounting), EN 14579 (metallic pressure equipment). Couplant selection critical above 500 kHz.
-   **Data Management:** MIMOSA/OSA-CBM standards enable interoperability between oil labs, AE systems, and CMMS platforms.
-   **Alert Thresholds:** Establish baselines over 3–6 months of healthy operation. Use statistical process control (Module 155) on trend data rather than absolute OEM limits.
