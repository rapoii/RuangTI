# Module 195: Technology Roadmapping (TRM) & Delphi Method

## 1. Introduction to Technology Roadmapping in Industrial Engineering

Technology Roadmapping (TRM) is a strategic planning tool used by Industrial Engineers to align technology development with business and market needs. Originally developed in the 1980s for semiconductor and aerospace industries, TRM provides a visual framework for identifying technology gaps, prioritizing R&D investments, and making informed technology adoption decisions.

For Industrial Engineers, TRM is essential in:
-   Technology selection and portfolio management
-   R&D planning and resource allocation
-   Product development roadmapping
-   Industry 4.0 and digital transformation initiatives

## 2. The Technology Roadmapping Process

### 2.1 Core Components of a TRM
A typical TRM includes:
-   **Business/Market Drivers:** Customer needs, market trends, competitive landscape
-   **Technology Needs:** Technical requirements derived from business drivers
-   **Technology Portfolio:** Existing technologies and gaps
-   **Technology Roadmap:** Timeline for development, acquisition, or retirement
-   **Dependencies:** Technical, organizational, and market dependencies

### 2.2 TRM Development Steps
1.  **Define Scope and Purpose:** Identify what the roadmap covers (product, process, technology, or enterprise).
2.  **Identify Stakeholders:** Include cross-functional teams (R&D, marketing, operations, finance).
3.  **Gather Inputs:** Market analysis, technology forecasts, customer interviews.
4.  **Develop the Roadmap:** Create the visual TRM with time axes (horizontal) and technology axes (vertical).
5.  **Validate and Iterate:** Review with stakeholders and refine based on feedback.
6.  **Implement and Monitor:** Integrate into strategic planning and update periodically.

## 3. The Delphi Method for Expert Consensus

The Delphi Method is a structured communication technique for gathering expert opinions to reach a consensus. Developed by the RAND Corporation in the 1950s, it is ideal for Industrial Engineering when expert consensus is needed for uncertain or complex decisions.

### 3.1 Delphi Method Process

**Round 1 (Idea Generation):**
-   Experts anonymously provide independent inputs
-   Collect and synthesize responses

**Round 2 (Refinement):**
-   Share synthesized responses with experts
-   Experts revise their views based on group input

**Round 3+ (Convergence):**
-   Continue rounds until consensus is reached (typically 2–4 rounds)
-   Final round for validation and commitment

### 3.2 Delphi Process in Industrial Engineering Applications
-   **Technology Assessment:** Prioritize emerging technologies (e.g., Industry 4.0 readiness)
-   **Risk Assessment:** Identify and weight potential disruptions
-   **Market Forecasting:** Consensus on future market sizes and trends
-   **Supplier Selection:** Expert evaluation of supplier capabilities

### 3.3 Delphi Advantages in IE
-   Reduces individual bias through anonymity
-   Leverages collective expertise
-   Facilitates cross-functional communication
-   Provides defensible, auditable decisions

## 4. Integration of TRM and Delphi Method

### 4.1 Delphi-Enhanced TRM Workflow
1.  Use Delphi to establish technology priorities and feasibility assessments
2.  Feed Delphi results into TRM development
3.  Use TRM to communicate technology strategies to stakeholders
4.  Update both TRM and Delphi results periodically based on new data

### 4.2 Example: Industry 4.0 Technology Roadmap
**Technology Drivers (Delphi-derived):**
-   Smart factory integration (High priority)
-   Predictive maintenance (High priority)
-   Digital twin technology (Medium-High)
-   Cobot integration (Medium)

**TRM Structure:**
-   **Short-term (0–2 years):** Implement basic IoT sensors, establish data infrastructure
-   **Medium-term (2–5 years):** Deploy predictive analytics, begin digital twin development
-   **Long-term (5+ years):** Full autonomous manufacturing, AI-driven optimization
